# Eye Rest competitor and review research

Research snapshot: **16 September 2026**

## Executive conclusion

Eye Rest should not compete by claiming the largest exercise count or by promising to
reverse myopia. Its strongest position is a **private, evidence-calibrated eye-comfort
and guided-practice app**: useful wellness behaviours are easy to start, clinical tools
are clearly separated, and every important limitation is visible.

The live review evidence says the most valuable missing feature was background break
reminders. That gap is now implemented. The app already addresses most other recurring
complaints: it has no forced account, ads or subscription wall; it works offline; it has
voice and haptic guidance; target speed is visible in Settings and in a running visual
drill; sessions can pause or skip; custom routines can change phase duration; and progress
is stored locally.

No competitor exercise was added just because it had a different name. The existing
library already covers the distinct exercise families. Duplicating “zooming,” “focus
change” and “pencil push-up,” for example, would inflate the catalogue without adding a
new stimulus. See [GLOBAL_EXERCISE_RESEARCH.md](../GLOBAL_EXERCISE_RESEARCH.md) for the
exercise-by-exercise evidence review.

## Scope and method

This was a qualitative review-mining pass, not a statistically representative sentiment
study. It used current public product pages and the review samples those stores exposed,
plus one review aggregator to catch recent reviews not shown in Google Play's featured
sample. Ratings vary by country, device and date. Reviewers' health outcomes are personal
reports, not proof that an exercise changed vision.

The closest products were selected by overlapping jobs-to-be-done: eye-strain comfort,
guided exercise, vision-therapy-style drills, 20-20-20 reminders, progress, and at-home
visual checks.

## Review themes

### What people repeatedly praise

1. **Immediate simplicity.** A useful session starts in seconds and lasts one to five
   minutes.
2. **Guidance without reading.** Voice, sound, vibration and clear animation matter when
   the user's eyes should be closed or looking away.
3. **Control.** People want to change target speed, duration, repetitions, schedule and
   workout contents.
4. **Habit support.** Background reminders, work-hour windows, widgets/watch cues and
   visible history turn advice into a routine.
5. **Perceived comfort.** Many reviews report less fatigue, dryness, tension or headache.
   This supports a comfort-oriented product promise, not a claim of refractive change.
6. **Games for the right audience.** Playful feedback helps some children and casual
   users stay engaged; adults in clinical care often prefer precise, diagnosis-specific
   controls.

### What people repeatedly criticize

1. Ads interrupting an exercise, confusing subscriptions and features removed after
   payment.
2. Forced sign-up for a local timer and unclear data collection/deletion.
3. Reminders failing in the background, moving after time changes, ignoring work hours or
   resetting when the app opens.
4. Important controls hidden inside the exercise or not offered at all.
5. Vague instructions, especially for movements that cannot be represented well on a
   phone.
6. Progress not saving, levels not advancing, targets leaving the screen, stutter or
   motion blur.
7. Exercise catalogues that look large but repeat the same stimulus.
8. Unsupported promises to improve acuity, reverse refractive error or treat a diagnosed
   condition without assessment.

## Changes implemented from this research

### Background screen-break reminders

- Opt-in notification permission requested only when the user enables reminders.
- Four interval choices: 20, 30, 45 or 60 minutes.
- Editable start/end hours and a weekdays-only option.
- Local scheduling; no account, server or personal identifier.
- One future alarm at a time, recalculated from actual delivery to avoid a pile-up.
- Rebuilt after device reboot, app replacement, manual clock changes and time-zone changes.
- Notification tells the user to look at a **real** distant object, rather than treating the
  phone screen as a distance target.
- Uses Android's battery-respecting inexact alarm API. Android documents that such alarms
  are never delivered before the requested time but can be delayed by system power
  management; the setting explains this rather than promising exact delivery.
- Pure schedule logic is covered by tests for disabled state, work hours, Friday-to-Monday
  rollover, weekend mode and invalid stored values.

## Recommended next releases

### P1 — strengthen outcomes without medical overclaiming

1. Add a two-tap **before/after comfort check-in** (dryness, strain, headache; 0-10). Show
   personal trends as observations, never as proof of improved eyesight.
2. Add **history export and backup** in a user-controlled format. Keep local-only as the
   default and do not require an account.
3. Add reminder actions for **Done**, **Snooze 10 min** and **Pause for today**. Measure
   reminder completion separately from merely posting a notification.
4. Add an automated test that renders each moving target at phone/tablet extremes and
   asserts its full bounding box stays on-screen.

### P2 — reach and retention

1. Home-screen widget showing next break and a one-tap quick session.
2. Wear OS companion for discreet haptic reminders; keep phone exercises on the larger
   display.
3. Localization beginning with Hindi and the next largest store languages. Voice scripts
   need native review, not machine translation alone.
4. A clinician-shareable routine code that imports prescribed components without letting
   the consumer quiz prescribe clinical drills.

### Explicitly do not copy

- “Restore eyesight,” “reverse myopia,” “prevent presbyopia,” or guaranteed acuity claims.
- Hundreds of renamed duplicates used only to inflate catalogue size.
- Ads between phases of a session.
- Forced account creation for reminders or local progress.
- Automatic clinical treatment plans for diplopia, amblyopia, strabismus, concussion or
  vestibular symptoms without professional assessment.
- Exact-alarm special access for a wellness nudge; it is disproportionate and costs
  battery/trust.
