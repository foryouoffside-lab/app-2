# Daily drill personalization: evidence and product rules

Research reviewed 15 September 2026. This is a product evidence note, not a medical
prescription or a substitute for professional review before release.

## What the daily set should be based on

The plan should not be based on a single invented "eye age". There is no validated
consumer eye-age number that can safely prescribe exercises. The inputs have different
jobs:

1. **Current symptoms are the main selector.** Dry/gritty/burning symptoms can select
   blink work. Strain after sustained near work can select a real distance break. Tension
   can select a clearly labelled comfort-only variation. Double vision does not select a
   teaming drill; it pauses automation and points to assessment.
2. **Screen time changes exposure priority.** Sustained screen use makes a break relevant.
   It does not mean the eye muscles are weak. More than 4-5 hours/day and poor ergonomics
   are associated with higher digital-eye-strain scores, but the evidence is heterogeneous
   and does not establish a universal safe limit.
3. **Human age is a modifier, not a diagnosis.** It is used for child outdoor guidance
   and to avoid assuming that the same near-focus demand suits every age. Age does not
   turn into an "eye age" or a visual-acuity promise.
4. **Glasses and contact lenses are modifiers.** Glasses do not need their own exercise.
   Uncorrected or partly corrected refractive error can contribute to symptoms, so the
   plan can prompt a working-distance prescription check. Contact-lens evidence in digital
   eye strain remains limited; contact wear only changes the explanation when dryness is
   already reported, rather than diagnosing dry eye by itself.
5. **Diagnosed conditions restrict automation.** Convergence insufficiency, amblyopia,
   strabismus and neuro-rehabilitation procedures remain clinician-directed. A diagnosis
   answer never unlocks those drills automatically.
6. **Red flags override all other inputs.** Sudden vision change/loss, new flashes or a
   curtain, severe pain, a painful red eye, sudden double vision, recent surgery or injury
   pause the daily set.

## What is included automatically

| Input | Automatic choice | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Reported dryness or diagnosed dry-eye/lid disease | Complete blink + gentle squeeze | A 2025 dose-finding study found 15 close-squeeze-open cycles, three times daily for two weeks reduced symptoms, incomplete blinks and conjunctival staining. Several measures did not improve and gains mostly returned to baseline after stopping. |
| Sustained screen use, screen strain or near/far blur | One 20-second real-distance break | Small uncontrolled intervention: short-term symptom reduction, without improvement in most measured ocular-surface/binocular signs. It is a behavior reminder, not muscle therapy. |
| Diagnosed lid-surface disease | Warm compress may rotate; lid hygiene remains a daily real-world action | TFOS DEWS II supports staged, subtype-specific management but also notes that many dry-eye treatments lack strong Level 1 evidence. Heat delivery matters and the app cannot measure it. |
| Child/adolescent | Outdoor daylight habit | A cluster-randomized school trial found lower three-year myopia incidence after adding 40 minutes outdoors to the school day. It did not reverse existing myopia. |
| Glasses or near/far blur | Rotating correction check | Uncorrected/undercorrected refraction can contribute to symptoms; trials of special computer lenses are low or very-low certainty. The app prompts an examination rather than recommending a lens. |
| Periocular tension | Palming or range-of-motion comfort variation | Comfort-only, low-specificity evidence. Never described as improving acuity, refraction or eye health. |

A symptom-free person with less than two hours of daily screen exposure can receive a
rest day. Adding drills merely to keep a streak has no demonstrated preventive benefit.

## Rotation and repetition

- The local calendar day is the stable seed, so reopening the app does not reshuffle the
  plan and a new plan appears after local midnight.
- Core items may repeat only when the relevant regimen or exposure itself repeats. For
  example, evidence-based blink practice is intentionally repeated for reported dryness.
- Optional tension work alternates between eligible choices on consecutive days.
- One non-core real-world action rotates through setup, blink awareness and correction
  check without repeating until the eligible pool has been used. Small pools include
  rest days, so a lone eligible action is not misleadingly shown every day as "variety".
- Variety never substitutes a lower-evidence drill for a symptom-matched core action.
- Clinical, amblyopia and neuro drills are excluded from the automatic pool even though
  they remain visible in Training Studio with their evidence and supervision status.

## Claims the product must not make

- Eye exercises remove glasses, improve refractive error or reverse myopia.
- Blue-light filters prevent digital eye strain.
- A general screen drill treats convergence insufficiency, amblyopia, strabismus or
  post-concussion dysfunction.
- A phone can diagnose dry eye, measure blink quality, verify a lens prescription or
  reproduce calibrated clinical vergence equipment.

The 2024 systematic review of controlled trials concluded that eye exercises have limited
to no efficacy for preventing or controlling myopia. This is why outdoor exposure is a
child prevention habit and not presented as an eyesight exercise.
